1. The Scientific Method

The scientific method is a logical process used to explore observations and answer questions. Whether you are figuring out why a Minecraft server crashed or testing how sunlight affects plants, you are using these steps.

The Core Steps:

  1. Ask a Question: Based on an observation (e.g., "Why does my custom scooter lose battery faster in the cold?").
  2. Research: Gather background information on the topic.
  3. Form a Hypothesis: An educated, testable prediction.
  4. Test with an Experiment: Design a fair test to check your hypothesis.
  5. Analyze Data: Look at the numbers, charts, and results.
  6. Draw a Conclusion: Was your hypothesis correct? If not, try again!

2. Experimental Variables (MV, RV, CV)

For an experiment to be valid and fair, you must tightly control what changes and what stays exactly the same. We organize these into three main categories of variables.

MV (Manipulated Variable)

Also known as the Independent Variable. This is the ONE thing the scientist changes on purpose to test it. (Cause)

RV (Responding Variable)

Also known as the Dependent Variable. This is the data you measure or observe. It "responds" to the MV. (Effect)

CV (Controlled Variables)

Also known as Constants. These are all the other factors you keep exactly the same so they don't mess up your results. (Fair Test)

Example Experiment: Testing if fertilizer makes plants grow taller.
MV: Amount of fertilizer given.
RV: Height of the plant (in cm).
CV: Same type of plant, same amount of water, same sunlight, same dirt.

3. Writing a Strong Hypothesis

A hypothesis is not just a blind guess. It is a structured prediction linking your MV to your RV. In middle school science, we always format it using the "If... then... because..." structure.

"If [MV changes this way], then [RV will do this], because [scientific reasoning]."

Example: "If I add 10 grams of fertilizer to the plant, then it will grow taller than the plant with no fertilizer, because fertilizer provides extra nutrients like nitrogen that support cellular growth."
