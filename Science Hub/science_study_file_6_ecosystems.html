1. Organization of an Ecosystem

An ecosystem includes all the living and non-living things interacting in a specific area. We organize environments into different levels of scale, from smallest to largest:

  • Organism: A single living thing (e.g., one wolf).
  • Population: A group of the same species living in the same area (e.g., a pack of wolves).
  • Community: All the different populations living together (e.g., wolves, rabbits, trees, and birds in a forest).
  • Ecosystem: The community PLUS the non-living environment (e.g., the forest, including the dirt, weather, and river).
Biotic vs. Abiotic:
Biotic Factors are living things (plants, animals, bacteria).
Abiotic Factors are non-living things that affect life (sunlight, temperature, water, rocks).

2. Energy Flow: Food Chains & Webs

Energy in almost every ecosystem originates from the Sun. It flows through the ecosystem as organisms eat one another.

🌱 Producers (Autotrophs)

Organisms that make their own food using sunlight via photosynthesis (e.g., plants, algae, phytoplankton).

🐇 Primary Consumers

Herbivores that eat producers to get their energy (e.g., rabbits, deer, grasshoppers).

🦊 Secondary & Tertiary Consumers

Carnivores and omnivores that eat other consumers. Apex predators sit at the very top of this chain.

🍄 Decomposers

The "recyclers" of the ecosystem. They break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil (e.g., fungi, bacteria).

Note: A food chain shows one single path of energy. A food web is much more realistic because it shows all the overlapping and interconnected food chains in an ecosystem.

3. The Energy Pyramid & The 10% Rule

As you move up the food chain, energy is lost. An energy pyramid shows how much energy is available at each trophic level.

The 10% Rule

Only about 10% of the energy from one level is passed on to the next level up. The other 90% is used by the organism for living, growing, and escaping predators, or is lost to the environment as heat.

Why are apex predators so rare? Because so much energy is lost at each level, an ecosystem needs thousands of plants to support just a few herbivores, and hundreds of herbivores to support just one apex predator like an eagle or a bear!
