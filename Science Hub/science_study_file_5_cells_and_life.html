1. Cell Theory & Types of Cells

Cells are the microscopic building blocks of all living things. The Cell Theory states three things: All living things are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of structure and function, and all cells come from pre-existing cells.

"There are two main categories of cells: Prokaryotic (simple, no nucleus, like bacteria) and Eukaryotic (complex, have a nucleus, like plants and animals)."

Plant Cells vs. Animal Cells

While plant and animal cells share many of the same parts (like the nucleus and mitochondria), plant cells have a few extra structures to help them survive without moving.

Animal Cell: Flexible membrane, no cell wall.

Plant Cell: Rigid cell wall, large central vacuole, and chloroplasts.

2. The Organelles (Tiny Organs)

Eukaryotic cells are filled with highly specialized structures called organelles. Think of the cell like a tiny, self-sustaining city.

🧠 Nucleus

The "brain" or control center of the cell. It holds the DNA (genetic instructions) needed to build proteins and run the cell.

⚡ Mitochondria

The "powerhouse" of the cell. It performs cellular respiration, breaking down sugar (glucose) to create usable energy (ATP).

☀️ Chloroplasts

Found only in plant cells. These act like solar panels, using sunlight to perform photosynthesis and create glucose.

🧱 Cell Wall

Found only in plant cells (and fungi). A rigid outer layer made of cellulose that provides structure so plants can stand tall.

🛡️ Cell Membrane

The "security guard." A semi-permeable boundary that surrounds all cells and controls what enters and exits the cell.

💧 Vacuole

The "storage tank." Plant cells have one massive central vacuole to store water, while animal cells have several smaller ones.

3. Photosynthesis & Cellular Respiration

Plants and animals rely on each other in a continuous cycle of energy and matter. The waste products of one process are the exact ingredients needed for the other.

The Chemistry of Life

  • Photosynthesis (Happens in Chloroplasts): Plants take in Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$), Water ($H_2O$), and Sunlight to produce Glucose (Sugar) and Oxygen gas ($O_2$).
  • Cellular Respiration (Happens in Mitochondria): Animals (and plants) breathe in Oxygen and eat Glucose to produce usable energy (ATP), breathing out Carbon Dioxide and Water as waste.
