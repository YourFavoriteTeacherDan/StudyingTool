1. Renewable vs. Non-Renewable Resources

A natural resource is anything in nature that humans use for energy, building materials, or survival. We divide these resources into two major categories based on how fast nature can replace them.

☀️ Renewable Resources

Resources that can be naturally replenished at or faster than the rate at which they are consumed. Examples: Solar, wind, hydroelectric, and geothermal energy.

🛢️ Non-Renewable Resources

Resources that exist in limited amounts and take millions of years to form inside Earth's crust. Examples: Coal, oil (petroleum), natural gas, and uranium.

Fossil Fuels: Coal, oil, and natural gas are formed from ancient decomposed plants and marine organisms subjected to immense heat and pressure over hundreds of millions of years.

2. Human Impact on Ecosystems

As human populations grow, human activities alter ecosystems, landscapes, and atmospheric conditions globally.

Major Environmental Challenges:

  • Deforestation: Clearing forests for agriculture or urban development removes trees that naturally pull Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) out of the atmosphere.
  • Pollution & Ocean Acidification: Runoff from fertilizers causes algal blooms (eutrophication) in freshwater systems, while excess atmospheric carbon dissolves into ocean water, forming carbonic acid and harming marine life.
  • Greenhouse Effect: Gasses like $CO_2$ and Methane ($CH_4$) trap heat in Earth's atmosphere, leading to global warming and extreme climate events.

3. Conservation & Engineering Solutions

Scientists and engineers actively design systems to minimize environmental destruction while supporting human energy and infrastructure needs.

♻️ Sustainability

Using resources in a way that meets current human needs without depleting or permanently damaging the environment for future generations.

🌾 Biodiversity Conservation

Protecting the variety of life in an ecosystem. High biodiversity makes ecosystems more resilient against disease and natural disasters.
