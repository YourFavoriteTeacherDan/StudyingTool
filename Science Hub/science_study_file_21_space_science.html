1. Earth's Tilt & The Seasons

Seasons are NOT caused by Earth getting closer to or farther from the Sun in its orbit. Instead, seasons occur because Earth's axis is tilted at 23.5 degrees relative to its orbital plane.

☀️ Summer Solstice

Occurs when a hemisphere is tilted directly toward the Sun, receiving direct sunlight and experiencing longer daylight hours.

❄️ Winter Solstice

Occurs when a hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, receiving indirect sunlight spread over a larger surface area.

Equinox: Twice per year (Spring and Autumn), neither hemisphere is tilted toward or away from the Sun, resulting in equal 12-hour days and 12-hour nights everywhere on Earth.

2. Moon Phases & Gravity Tides

The Moon orbits Earth roughly every 27.3 days. As it moves, the portion of its sunlit side visible from Earth changes continuously, creating the 8 distinct lunar phases.

Tidal Forces

Gravitational pulls from the Moon and Sun distort Earth's oceans, creating ocean tides twice daily.

  • Spring Tides: Occur during New Moon and Full Moon when Sun, Earth, and Moon align in a straight line. High tides are extra high, low tides extra low.
  • Neap Tides: Occur during 1st and 3rd Quarter moons when Sun and Moon pull at 90-degree right angles, producing moderate tide differences.

3. Gravity & Planetary Motion

Gravity holds planets in orbit around stars and galaxies together. According to Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation, gravitational attraction depends on two key factors:

Gravity = (Mass 1 × Mass 2) / (Distance)²
  • Mass: Objects with greater mass exert stronger gravitational pull.
  • Distance: Increasing distance between objects rapidly weakens gravitational pull (inverse-square relationship).
