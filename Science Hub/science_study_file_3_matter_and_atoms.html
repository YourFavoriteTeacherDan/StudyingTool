1. What is Matter?

Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space (volume). It makes up everything in the universe, from the device you are reading this on to the stars in the sky.

Phase Changes

Matter shifts between phases when thermal energy (heat) is added or removed. This changes how fast the particles vibrate and how tightly they are bound together.

  • Solid: Particles are tightly packed and vibrate in place. Fixed shape and fixed volume.
  • Liquid: Particles have more energy and can slide past each other. Fixed volume, but takes the shape of its container.
  • Gas: Particles have high energy, move freely, and spread out to fill their entire container. No fixed shape or volume.
  • Plasma: Superheated gas where electrons are ripped away from the nucleus (e.g., lightning, neon signs, the Sun).

2. The Structure of an Atom

An atom is the smallest unit of ordinary matter that forms a chemical element. Every solid, liquid, gas, and plasma is composed of neutral or ionized atoms.

"The nucleus holds almost all of the atom's mass, but the electron cloud makes up almost all of the atom's volume. Atoms are 99.9999% empty space!"

Protons

Heavy particles found inside the nucleus. The number of protons determines what element the atom is (Atomic Number).

+1 Charge

Neutrons

Heavy particles found in the nucleus alongside protons. They act like glue to keep the nucleus from blowing apart.

0 Neutral

Electrons

Tiny, nearly massless particles orbiting the nucleus in shells. They are responsible for all chemical reactions.

-1 Charge

3. The Periodic Table

The Periodic Table organizes all known chemical elements based on their atomic number (number of protons) and chemical properties.

How to Read It:

  • Periods (Rows): Elements in the same row have the same number of electron orbital shells.
  • Groups (Columns): Elements in the same vertical column have similar chemical behaviors and the same number of valence (outer) electrons.
  • Atomic Number: The big number at the top of an element's square. It tells you exactly how many protons are in the nucleus.
  • Atomic Mass: The number at the bottom. Subtract the Atomic Number from the Atomic Mass to find the number of neutrons!
