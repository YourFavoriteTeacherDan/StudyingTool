1. Physical vs. Chemical Changes

Matter is constantly changing, but there is a fundamental difference between changing an object's appearance and changing its chemical identity.

🧊 Physical Change

Alters the form or appearance of matter without creating a new chemical substance. Usually reversible. Examples: Melting ice, tearing paper, dissolving salt in water.

🔥 Chemical Change (Reaction)

Occurs when chemical bonds break and form new arrangements, creating entirely new chemical substances with new properties. Examples: Rusting iron, burning wood, baking bread.

Signs of a Chemical Reaction: Unexpected color change, temperature change (exothermic or endothermic), gas production (bubbles), or formation of a precipitate (solid settling out of a liquid).

2. The Law of Conservation of Mass

Formulated by Antoine Lavoisier, this law states that matter cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. The total mass of the reactants MUST equal the total mass of the products.

Total Mass of Reactants = Total Mass of Products

Reactants vs. Products

In a chemical equation, the starting ingredients are called Reactants (left side of the arrow), and the resulting substances are called Products (right side of the arrow).

3. Balancing Chemical Equations

Because mass is conserved, a written chemical equation must have the exact same number of each type of atom on both sides of the arrow.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Atom Count:

  • Left Side (Reactants): 4 Hydrogen atoms, 2 Oxygen atoms
  • Right Side (Products): 4 Hydrogen atoms, 2 Oxygen atoms

Note: You can change the big numbers in front (coefficients), but you can NEVER change the tiny bottom numbers (subscripts)!
